# UVA EM Internal Voice

The register Matt uses inside the UVA Department of Emergency Medicine is distinct from his external / consulting voice. This skill captures those departmental conventions.

Layer this on top of `feedback_matt_email_voice.md` in auto-memory, which holds Matt's universal email defaults (warm opening, perspective markers, autonomy-preserving closes, transparent AI use). This skill adds the UVA-EM-specific overrides — most notably the "forwardable" standard and calibrated deference to senior clinicians.

This document is intentionally a living doc. Add conventions as they surface.

---

## Scope

Platform-agnostic. This skill applies to:

- Direct email to UVA EM colleagues (peer, senior, junior)
- Slack messages in UVA EM channels
- Resident-wide announcements
- Departmental announcements (HTML or plain text)
- Meeting briefs and internal planning documents that may circulate
- Any written artifact that an internal UVA EM reader might see

It does not apply to external communications (Red Cell, HIP, MDP external partners), which have their own style skills.

## Two modes — know which one you are in

### Mode A — Professional correspondence (1:1 or small group)

Applies to: direct emails, Slack DMs, small-group threads. Most of Matt's UVA EM writing is in this mode.

**Default standard: "Forwardable."** Every message should read as if it might be printed and slid across a table to Matt, or forwarded up to a Dean, Chair, or senior administrator. Write accordingly:

- Clear subject or opening that names the topic
- Formal salutations with colons for senior clinicians: **"Dear Dr. Muck:"** / **"Dear Dr. Taylor:"** — not commas, not "Hi Andy"
- For peers and colleagues Matt knows well: first-name salutation with comma is fine ("Bill," "Heather,")
- Complete sentences; no casual text-speak; no emojis
- Clean paragraph breaks
- Bold used sparingly — only when it helps a reader scan the document (e.g., surfacing a deadline, a decision point, or a named ask). If bolding does not aid scanning, do not bold.
- Never strident. Matt does not threaten, ultimatum, or apply pressure in writing. Even when pushing for a decision, the tone remains collegial and the ask is framed as a request, not a demand.
- Minimal formatting overall — no colored text, no subheadings, no decorative elements. Let the prose carry the work.

### Mode B — Announcements with visual structure

Applies to: HTML save-the-dates, research day comms, departmental rollouts, multi-recipient announcements where scannability matters more than letter-formality.

In this mode, visual structure is a feature, not a lapse in decorum:

- Bolding, color accents, and subheadings are welcome — they help readers scan.
- UVA brand colors (Jefferson Blue `#232D4B`, Cavalier Orange `#E57200`) when the piece is formally a UVA EM departmental document.
- Short sections with clear hierarchy (H2 / H3 / callout blocks).
- Still no "gang," "folks," or other casual group-address. Matt uses "team," "colleagues," "residents," "everyone," or just omits the group address entirely.
- The Research Day save-the-date is a reference point for this mode.

Err toward Mode A if uncertain. The bar for Mode B is "this message is genuinely designed as an announcement, not a conversation."

## Style specifics

### Salutations
- Senior clinicians: `Dear Dr. [Last]:`
- Peers and colleagues known well: `[First],`
- Residents (group): `Residents,` or `Team,` or `Colleagues,` — never `Hey gang` / `Hey folks`
- Admins (Penny, Paige): `[First],`

### Opening line
Per the universal email defaults (auto-memory), open with relational acknowledgment before the ask — gratitude, context, or acknowledgment of a recent exchange. For internal UVA EM, keep the warmth but avoid being overly familiar with senior clinicians.

### Perspective markers
Use "To me," "my thinking," "I think," "my proposed [thing]" when offering a framing or recommendation. These are present in UVA EM mode too, though slightly more formal phrasing is fine ("It seems to me that..." / "My view would be...").

### Bold
- Mode A: only to help scanning — a deadline, a named decision point, a specific ask.
- Mode B: more freely, to structure the scan.

### Formatting off-limits in Mode A
- Bullet points used as prose structure (one-sentence bullets stacked)
- Color text
- Emojis
- Subheadings
- Exclamation points in series (one is fine for genuine enthusiasm)

### Forbidden words / phrases
- "gang"
- "folks"
- "Hey team!" style openings for senior-facing documents
- Overly casual sign-offs ("cheers," "later," "-m")

### Preferred sign-offs
- To senior clinicians: `Thank you again, [Name]. / Matt` or `Best, / Matt` or `Respectfully, / Matt` (on genuinely formal matters)
- To peers: `Thanks, / Matt` or `Best, / Matt`
- To residents: `Thanks, / Matt` or `Thanks all, / Matt`

### Signature block
When the message is formal or the recipient may not know Matt's role, include:

```
Matt Trowbridge, MD
UVA Department of Emergency Medicine
```

Matt is comfortable naming Claude AI as a drafting tool when relevant ("I've worked with Claude AI to generate a draft outline"). This is a feature of his voice, not a concession. Do not hide it, and do not over-disclose — mention it when it clarifies the origin of an artifact, skip it otherwise.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Writing in a way that would embarrass Matt if forwarded to Andy Muck
- Applying pressure or deadlines with a strident tone
- "Hey gang" / "Hey folks" openings anywhere internal
- Bullet-heavy prose in Mode A — stacked bullets read as a to-do list, not a letter
- Bold-everywhere in Mode A
- Declarative assertions about what the recipient "should" think — always leave room for disagreement ("if this framing works," "your call")
- Sign-offs that feel performatively casual to senior clinicians

## Quick-reference: opening patterns

**To a senior clinician, making an ask (Mode A):**

```
Dear Dr. Taylor:

Thank you again for [recent exchange / their support / their time]. Following up on [specific thread] — [one-sentence frame].

[Body: the substantive ask, framed with "to me / my thinking" language, one or two paragraphs.]

[Close with a concrete next step and autonomy-preserving close: "happy to discuss by phone if easier," "fully your call," etc.]

Thank you again,
Matt
```

**To a peer / collaborator (Mode A):**

```
[First name],

Thanks for [context]. Here's my thinking...

[Body.]

[Concrete next step.]

Thanks,
Matt
```

**Departmental announcement (Mode B):**

```html
[H2 headline — the thing being announced]
[One-line what-this-is in plain prose]

**What / When / Where** (bolded labels + values as a scannable block)

[Short body paragraph — the "why" and the "what's expected"]

[Call to action with clear next step]
```
